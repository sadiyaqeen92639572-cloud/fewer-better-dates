Why Am I Still Single?

It's rarely one big flaw. Usually it's a smaller mix of things, and most of them are fixable once you can actually see them.

5 min readField notes from Fewer Better Dates

Everyone who's been single a while has run this question in their head at 1am: what's wrong with me? Then a friend gets engaged, or an ex settles down, and the question gets louder. Here's the more useful version of it: you're probably not doing anything wrong. You might just be stuck in a system that was never built to find you the right person.

You're comparing yourself to people who got lucky on timing

Relationships you see from the outside look effortless because you only ever see the part after it worked out. Nobody posts the eighteen months before that, the mismatched dates, the person who ghosted, the one who wanted something else entirely. Being single at 30, 35 or 45 isn't evidence that something's broken in you. It's evidence that the right match hasn't shown up yet, and matching is genuinely hard to get right by chance.

You've been on the apps too long to see them clearly

A few years of swiping trains you to evaluate people the way the app trains you to: fast, on looks and a couple of lines of text, with a hundred more profiles waiting behind this one. That's a terrible way to judge whether someone could be a good partner, and it's easy to walk away thinking the problem is the people, when the problem is the format.

Standards aren't the issue here. Judging someone in four seconds on a screen, then multiplying that by hundreds of people, is.

You might be waiting for a spark that isn't the real signal

Instant chemistry is a decent predictor of a good first date. It's a weak predictor of a good relationship.

A lot of long, stable relationships started as "fine, I guess" and grew from there. A lot of electric first dates went nowhere. If you're single because you keep waiting for a lightning-bolt feeling before giving someone a second date, you might be filtering out exactly the people who'd have been good for you.

Nobody around you is actually sorting for what you want

This is the part that's not really about you at all. Dating apps show you volume, not compatibility. Friends set you up with whoever's single, not whoever matches your values. Most of the machinery around modern dating optimises for quantity of options, and almost none of it optimises for whether two people actually want the same kind of relationship.

Being still single often just means: nobody has sorted for you yet. So you're doing the sorting yourself, one mismatched date at a time.
